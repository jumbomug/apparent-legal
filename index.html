How it works

Clear for parents. Concrete for kids.

  1. Set the day.

    Add a task from Today: type its name and press Return. Choose which tasks you want to check, and what stars they earn.

  2. Do one thing.

    Your child sees the next step, holds the button when it's done, and the Daily Path fills in dot by dot.

  3. Review if needed.

    Approve, send it back with a note, or excuse it for today. Face ID or an approval PIN keeps approvals yours.

  4. Rewards update.

    Stars land in the balance. The Daily Unlock opens. Both screens agree.

What we left out

Built on research, which meant leaving things out.

We read the published research on rewards and motivation before we built the loop. Most of what it told us was what not to do.

  • No streaks.A missed day is a missed day, not a loss.

  • No algorithm setting the stakes.You decide what a task is worth. Finn never hands out stars.

Two rhythms, no hidden math

Stars for rewards. Unlocks for the daily stuff.

Stars

Stars add up.

Stars build toward rewards your family picks: a movie night, choosing dinner, allowance. You choose which rewards need your OK.

Daily Unlock

An unlock opens once the day is handled.

A Daily Unlock is the privilege that opens once the everyday tasks are done, like Tech Time, and it relocks tomorrow. When a task counts toward it, the task earns no stars unless you give it some, so the privilege is the stake, not a prize on top.

Excused tasks count toward the unlock and earn no stars. Grades never touch stars or unlocks.

Screen Time, if you want it

Chosen apps can wait until the day is done.

A Daily Unlock can hold the apps you choose on your child's device until today's tasks are finished or excused. It uses Apple's Screen Time. The hold lifts for the rest of the day and comes back at midnight.

  • The hold runs on your child's device. When the last task is saved, the apps open there right away.
  • If the app can't tell where things stand, it leaves the hold as it was.
  • The apps you choose never leave your child's device.

What you'll need

  • Your child's own iPhone or iPad.
  • For a hold your child can't switch off without you, your child's Apple ID in your Family Sharing group.
  • Without Family Sharing, you can still turn it on. We'll tell you plainly that whoever holds the device can turn it off in Settings.
  • Or skip it. A Daily Unlock works with no apps held at all, as a family agreement.

Real days

Built for real days.

Stuck has a button.

Two taps on your child's screen tell every grown-up they need help. Your child sees “Help is on the way.” It never counts against anyone.

Sent back, gently.

If something needs another try, your note travels with it. Your child reads “Your parent asked you to try this one again.” Nothing in it is red.

Life happens.

Sick day? Excuse a task for today. It still counts toward the Daily Unlock and earns no stars, so the math stays honest.

Parents and grades

Every parent or guardian, free, always.

Invite another grown-up and they see the same day you do. Two homes, a grandparent, a nanny: any number of adults can join a family, and none of them is ever a paid seat.

Grades, kept in their place.

You can jot a grade per subject so your child sees it on a quiet card. That's all it does. Grades never change stars, rewards, or unlocks, and Apparent doesn't connect to any school system.

Privacy

Your family's day is not ad data.

  • No ads. No third-party analytics or tracking SDKs. We don't track you across apps or websites.
  • We never sell or rent your data.
  • Children never create accounts or passwords. You connect their device with a short-lived code.
  • We ask your permission before your child's device connects, and you can withdraw it any time.
  • Age groups instead of birthdates. No GPS or precise location.
  • Notifications stay generic: no child names, no task details.
  • Delete a child, or your whole account, right in the app.

Questions

Questions families ask.

Does my child need an account?

No. Children never create accounts or passwords. You add your child by name and school level, give permission, and connect their device with a short-lived code or a QR code.

What devices do we need?

An iPhone or iPad for you, and an iPhone or iPad for your child. Apparent is on the App Store, iOS only, and available in the United States.

What does Apparent cost?

Apparent is free for one child and every parent or guardian. Every new household gets a free first month with everything included: no payment details, nothing to cancel. After it, the free plan keeps the daily loop for every child you've added. Adding another child needs the Family plan, monthly or yearly, which covers every child. The App Store shows the prices.

Do I have to approve everything?

No. You choose which tasks you want to check. Everything else completes when your child holds the button.

What's the difference between stars and a Daily Unlock?

Stars add up and get spent on rewards your family picks. A Daily Unlock is a daily privilege, like Tech Time, that opens when chosen tasks are handled and relocks tomorrow. When a task counts toward an unlock, it earns no stars unless you give it some.

Can rewards be something other than screen time?

Yes. Rewards are whatever your family decides: a movie night, choosing dinner, an outing, allowance. The app never assumes what the reward is.

How does the Screen Time hold work, and is it required?

It's optional. A Daily Unlock can hold apps you choose on your child's device until today's tasks are done. It uses Apple's Screen Time, and you set it up on your child's device behind a parent check. For the version a child can't switch off without you, your child's Apple ID needs to be in your Family Sharing group.

What if the Wi-Fi is down in the morning?

You'll still see the day as it was last saved. Finishing a task, approving one, and claiming a reward need a connection, and Apparent doesn't queue changes for later. The hold runs on your child's device and keeps its schedule offline. If the app can't tell where things stand, it leaves the hold as it was, so held apps stay held until the device reconnects.

Will Apple's Downtime block Apparent itself?

It can. If you use Downtime, add Apparent to Always Allowed on your child's device. The app walks you through it.

What does the Help button do?

Your child taps it, then taps Ask for help, and every grown-up in the family with notifications on gets a notification. Your child sees “Help is on the way.” It never counts against them.

What happens on a sick day?

Excuse the task for today. It still counts toward the Daily Unlock and earns no stars.

Can both parents use it? What if we're separated?

Yes. Any number of parents or guardians can join a family, and none of them is ever a paid seat. Everyone sees the same day.

Who is Finn?

Finn is the calm teal character on your child's screen, drawn by Zac Schmidt, who created all of Apparent's art. Finn keeps your child company, never judges, never hands out stars, and never speaks on serious screens.

Does Apparent connect to our school?

No. You can jot grades by hand so your child sees them on a quiet card. Grades never affect rewards.

Is there an Android version?

Not yet. Apparent is iOS only.

Why do you say kids tend to lose interest around week three?

Because they do, with most systems like this. We'd rather say so.
